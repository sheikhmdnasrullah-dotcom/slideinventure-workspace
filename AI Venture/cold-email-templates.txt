SLIDEIN VENTURE — COLD EMAIL TEMPLATES (6 ANGLES)
The 100-Appointment Guarantee
==================================================

HOW TO USE THIS FILE
--------------------
Six templates below, each built around a different research angle. Pick the
one that actually fits what you find on the prospect — don't force template 1
on someone who clearly fits template 4. Every bracket must be filled with
something REAL you found, or don't send it. A guessed or generic bracket is
worse than no personalization at all — it reads as fake immediately.

Before writing to anyone, spend 10 minutes finding:
- One specific, provable detail from their content, site, or profile (a
  timestamp, a phrase they used, a specific claim, a specific offer). Vague
  ("they talk about marketing") does not count. Specific ("in the March 12
  video around minute 4 they said referrals were 'soul-crushing' to rely on")
  does.
- What they actually sell and at what rough price point.
- Whether they currently do any outbound, and if so, whether it looks
  broken (inconsistent, generic, or abandoned).
- One thing that would obviously move the needle for them if fixed.

Every template keeps the same skeleton on purpose — it is what makes the
originals sound like you:
  1. Respect their time up front.
  2. A genuine, specific observation (not flattery, not generic praise).
  3. What you do, framed as an outcome, not a task list.
  4. The guarantee line — 100 booked appointments in 90 days, or you keep
     working free until they get there.
  5. Two ways to respond: a quick call, or a 90-second video.
  6. A short, honest closing line. Sign only "Tanim."

Never break these rules across any template:
- No em dashes.
- No "I hope this email finds you well," "I came across your profile,"
  "quick question," "circle back," "leverage," "streamline," "synergy."
- No mention of AI, automation, dashboards, or "systems" as the headline —
  lead with the outcome (appointments, clients, calls), not the mechanism.
- One CTA path offered as two options (call or video) — never more than that.
- Sign off only as "Tanim." No brand name, no "we," no team language.

NOTES FOR ROTATION / A-B USE
==================================================
- These are angles, not a sequence. Send one per prospect based on what you
  actually find, not in a fixed order.
- If none of the six angles genuinely fit a prospect, don't force one, do
  more research or skip them. A forced angle reads as generic no matter how
  well the template is written.
- Keep every sent email under 180 words. If a filled-in bracket makes it
  run long, cut elsewhere, never the guarantee line or the two response
  paths.
- Track which angle books the most replies per ICP segment (referral-only
  vs. already-doing-outbound vs. burned-by-agency) and lean into whichever
  wins for that segment.
