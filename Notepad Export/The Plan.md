## Part 0: Read this first

You handed me a real system, not a vague idea. Ten components, a clear guarantee, a genuine original piece (the deanonymize-and-personalize loop), and a stated boundary (no paid boosting, no copying the course wholesale). That is more than most people bring me. So I read all eight skills you named and I actually ran the numbers instead of just cheering it on. Four things need to be said before we build anything, because they will hurt you in month four if we skip them now.

**Correction 1: there is no recurring revenue anywhere in this plan.
**Your real estate playbook's entire insight was "sell an installation, not your time." This new sketch throws that away. $5,000 for 50 appointments or $10,000 for 100 appointments is a project fee. Once you hit the number, the engagement is over unless you say otherwise. That means every single month you are back to zero, hunting for the next $5k-$10k client, exactly like the video editing agency you are trying to leave behind. The fix is not complicated: keep everything you have built here, and once the guaranteed appointments are delivered, roll the client onto a flat monthly fee to keep the system running (dashboard, chatbot, phone agent, ongoing content, ongoing outbound). You already know this pattern works. Use it here too.

**Correction 2: your price is below the market floor, and your guarantee has no ceiling on your downside.
**I checked what qualified appointment setting actually sells for in 2026. Pay-per-appointment agencies charge $150 to $400 a meeting. BANT-verified, properly qualified appointments run $400 to $750. Your $5,000-for-50 and $10,000-for-100 both work out to exactly $100 per appointment, before you have delivered a single video, chatbot, phone agent, or research-matched email. You are underpricing a bigger deliverable than almost anyone else in this market sells, and then attaching an uncapped guarantee ("I will work for free until then") on top of it. There is no ceiling on how many months of free labor one slow client can cost you. That combination, cheap price plus unlimited downside plus the biggest deliverable list in the category, is the single riskiest offer shape you can build. Part 7 rebuilds this with a cap and a real definition of "qualified" so a bad-faith client cannot trap you.

**Correction 3: "no boosting" is a real bet against real numbers, and you should know the odds before you commit to it.
**Verified 2026 data on AI-avatar content: click-through rate runs roughly half of real human UGC (0.9 to 1.3 percent versus 1.8 to 2.4 percent), and AI avatar content converts 20 to 25 percent lower than real UGC specifically on retargeting. The advantage AI avatars do have is speed: brands running AI avatars alongside real creators find winning hooks two to three times faster because they can test more variations per week. That is the honest trade you are making by refusing paid boosting and leaning on 90 organic AI-avatar videos: you get to iterate fast and cheap, but organic reach on synthetic-presenter content is a real uphill climb, not a guaranteed viral mechanism. Path A and D (real footage, real presenter) in your own menu exist for exactly this reason. Do not let every client default into path B just because it is zero effort for them.

**Correction 4: your AI phone receptionist and your deanonymization engine are not the same legal situation.
**Emailing someone identified through Warmly or Bullseye is generally fine under CAN-SPAM if you disclose, give a real opt-out, and honor it. Placing an AI voice call, or sending an automated text, to that same person's phone number is a different law. TCPA treats an AI voice as an "artificial voice" requiring prior express written consent, exactly like a robocall, and this applies regardless of whether the number came from a form fill or a de-anonymization tool. A visit to a website is not consent to be called. So the phone receptionist in your stack should answer inbound calls and call back people who already called in or explicitly opted in on a form, the same rule your real estate playbook already got right. It should not proactively dial numbers your research engine surfaced. Route those through email and let a reply or a booked call be the consent event that unlocks a phone conversation.

None of this means the idea is bad. It means the idea needs the fixes below before you put a client's money against it. Let's build it properly, skill by skill.

## Part 1: Positioning (obviously-awesome)

**Competitive alternatives.** If you did not exist, a business chasing more booked calls would do one of four things: do nothing and rely on word of mouth, hire a human SDR or a virtual assistant, buy point-solution software and stitch it together themselves (a Warmly subscription plus Instantly plus a chatbot widget plus a video tool), or hire a full-service growth agency that runs paid ads for them. Every one of these is a real alternative you are being compared against, whether the prospect says so or not.

**Unique attributes, checked against the "only we" test:**

- Only you combine organic content, visitor deanonymization, ICP matching, and hyper-personalized outbound into one owned system, instead of selling one link in that chain the way Warmly, an SDR tool, or a UGC agency each do alone.
- Only you attach a performance guarantee measured in booked appointments rather than in emails sent, videos posted, or leads generated. Most of the tools above sell activity. You are proposing to sell an outcome.
- Only you (so far) are combining a video-editing background with this stack, meaning your path A/D content can be genuinely well produced rather than templated AI slop, which is where most competitors in this exact genre are weakest.
**Value themes:** fewer vendors to manage, one dashboard instead of five logins, a number on a contract instead of a vague promise, and a system the client keeps operating after you leave (if you carry the "you keep the system" principle over from your real estate playbook, which you should).

**Best-fit customer: this is currently undefined, and it is the biggest hole in the plan.** You wrote "ICP definition" as backend step one of your delivery process, which is correct for each client, but you have not defined your own ICP, meaning who Tanim goes and sells this to first. A grand slam offer aimed at "everyone" converts nobody. Borrow the logic from your own real estate playbook: pick businesses where one closed customer is worth $3,000 or more, where the buyer already spends money on lead generation, and where you can reach 500+ of them through a channel you control. Property managers, B2B service agencies, and info/coaching businesses with a $2k+ program all fit that filter. Pick one to launch with. You can widen later.

**Market category.** Do not position this as "an appointment setting agency." That category is commoditized, it is where the $100-per-appointment price gets compared against $150 to $750 competitors and looks cheap in the wrong way, and it invites "all you do is call people" objections. Position it as a subcategory: **an installed growth system with a booked-appointment guarantee**, closer to how your real estate playbook frames itself, not a services retainer. The category should make the client compare you to hiring a growth team, not to a call center.

**Positioning statement (draft):** "For [service businesses with $2k+ customer value], the inns orgstalled growth system that turanic content and researched leads into booked calls, guaranteed, without you having to run a single paid ad."

## Part 4: What actually checks out (deep-research)

I ran this section for real rather than assuming your source material was accurate. Three findings matter enough to change your build.

**The three lead-capture tools are all real and correctly categorized.** [Retention.com](https://Retention.com) is a genuine identity-resolution platform, but it is built for e-commerce and DTC brands doing high-volume Shopify traffic, matching anonymous shoppers against a 500-million-record identity graph, not a B2B tool. Warmly and Bullseye are both real B2B visitor-identification and intent platforms, matching website traffic to named individuals and companies (Bullseye advertises up to 40 percent person-level identification, Warmly around 60 percent company-level and 15 percent person-level). Your instinct to slot [Retention.com](https://Retention.com) under B2C and Warmly/Bullseye under B2B was correct. Keep that split.

**Deanonymization sits in a genuine, active legal gray zone, separate from the TCPA issue in Part 0.** Recent US lawsuits treat undisclosed visitor tracking as "wiretapping" under state laws like California's CIPA. Reputable vendors handle this with consent banners, geofencing (excluding EU traffic, which GDPR treats far more strictly), and short data-retention windows. Before you resell this to clients, confirm which vendor tier you're on actually ships those protections, and put a clear cookie-consent notice on every client landing page, not just a privacy policy buried in the footer.

**The 90-day guarantee's economics need a real cost model before you price it.** Warmly and Bullseye both gate meaningful volume behind paid tiers once you're past a free allotment (a handful of free reveals, then credits or a monthly subscription). Add AI voice/avatar generation credits, cold email infrastructure, and your own labor across 90 videos plus researched emails for every matched lead, and the true cost to deliver one $5,000 client is very likely well above the $320-per-install figure from your real estate playbook, because this offer has no snapshot-and-load shortcut yet. Do not assume this scales the same way until you've built and timed one client end to end.

## Part 5: The psychology underneath the mechanics (marketing-psychology)

- **The content engine runs on the mere exposure effect and the availability heuristic.** Ninety videos work less because any single one goes viral and more because repeated, familiar exposure builds trust, and a steady stream of case-study-style content makes "this works" easy to imagine. Judge the content plan by consistency, not by hoping for a breakout hit.
- **The hyper-personalized outbound email is powered by reciprocity and the IKEA effect, working in reverse.** When a prospect can tell real research went into the email (a specific detail about their business, not a merge tag), they feel a small social debt and credit you with more effort than a template would earn. This is your single highest-leverage psychological asset and it is also the most fragile: one generic-feeling email breaks the illusion for that entire sequence.
- **The landing page and chatbot should obey Hick's Law.** One clear action (book a call) beats three options every time. Resist the urge to give the chatbot five things it can do; give it one job (qualify and book) and let it escalate anything outside that job to a human.
- **The guarantee itself is a loss-aversion instrument, and you are currently using it at maximum, uncapped strength.** A strong guarantee is supposed to make "yes" feel safe for the buyer. It should not simultaneously make "yes" feel unlimited-risk for you. Part 7 fixes the wording so the psychology still works on the client without exposing you to indefinite free labor.
- **Disclose the AI, deliberately, every time.** Your real estate playbook already found that disclosing "this is an AI assistant" tests better than hiding it, because getting caught pretending destroys trust instantly. The 2026 AI-avatar backlash data in Part 4 confirms this is now a live risk, not a theoretical one. Label AI-generated video content on every platform that requires it (TikTok and Meta both do), and have the chatbot and phone agent open by identifying themselves as AI.

## Part 6: Is this ready to sell yet (product-market-fit)

Be honest with yourself here: you have zero clients on this exact version of the offer, so there is no PMF signal to report, positive or negative. That is fine. It just means the next move is a validation pass, not a scaling pass.

**Sean Ellis question to run after your first 5 to 10 pilot clients complete a full cycle:** "How would you feel if you could no longer use this system?" Very disappointed / somewhat disappointed / not disappointed. 40 percent or more saying "very disappointed" is your PMF signal. Below that, don't add more videos or more channels, fix what's actually broken first.

**Watch these signals instead of vanity metrics** (views, videos posted, emails sent):

- Reply-to-positive-response rate on the personalized outbound (this tells you if the research angle is actually landing)
- Show-rate on booked appointments (a high book rate with a low show rate means the qualification step is weak, not the outreach)
- Whether a client converts from the guarantee period into an ongoing retainer once you add one (Correction 1). That single number tells you more about real fit than anything else on this list.
**Pivot menu if the pilot underperforms:** if organic content isn't producing appointments, that's a channel pivot, lean harder on the deanonymization-plus-outbound loop and treat video as a trust-building supplement, not the primary driver. If outbound personalization isn't landing, that's a need pivot, the research depth may need to go deeper (recent funding news, a specific pain point from a recent post) rather than wider (more leads processed shallowly).

## Part 7: Rebuilding the offer itself (offers + hundred-million-offers)

**Value equation, scored honestly:**

**The binding constraint is perceived likelihood, not price.** An uncapped guarantee sounds strong but reads as either naive or fake to a buyer who has been burned by agency promises before, especially at a price already below market. Fix likelihood with a named, bounded guarantee and, once you have them, two or three real numbers from pilot clients, not with a bigger promise.

**Rebuilt guarantee, answering your own open fine print questions directly:**

- **Qualified appointment, defined:** the prospect confirms budget or buying authority exists, confirms the stated need matches your client's offer, and confirms a specific date and time on your client's calendar. Log all three per appointment in the dashboard so "qualified" is never a debate after the fact.
- **No-shows:** count toward the guarantee number if the appointment was confirmed qualified at time of booking and a reminder sequence was sent. You are not responsible for someone else's calendar discipline once you've done your job correctly.
- **Approval-delay clock pause:** the 90-day clock pauses for any day the client hasn't approved copy, video assets, or given you access they were supposed to provide within 48 hours of your request. Put this in the contract before day one, not after a client stalls you for three weeks and then blames the timeline.
- **The uncapped part gets capped:** "we work free until you hit the number" becomes "we work free for up to an additional 60 days past the original 90 to hit the number; if we still haven't hit it by then, [refund the difference / offer a reduced-scope makegood / renegotiate]." Pick one. An honestly bounded guarantee that you can actually honor beats an unlimited one that quietly becomes a resentment machine six months in.
**Bonus stack, using what you already built.** You don't need to invent new bonuses, name the ones already in your ten-part list as bonuses in the sales conversation instead of burying them in an "everything included" list: name the dashboard separately with its own value ("Command Center: see and direct every lead in one place"), name the guarantee separately ("The 100-Call Guarantee"), name the researched-lead engine separately ("Buyer Radar"). Named components feel more real and more valuable than the same components listed as bullet points under one heading.

**Naming, using MAGIC (Magnetic reason, Avatar, Goal, Indicate timeframe, Container):**

- *The 90-Day Booked Call Guarantee*
- *Buyer Radar: 100 Qualified Calls in 90 Days, Guaranteed*
- *The Installed Pipeline System*
**Price and payment structure, once the ICP is chosen (Part 1):** raise the per-appointment price toward the verified $150-$400 standard range once you have even one case study, keep the 50/100 tiers but consider a third tier that mirrors your real estate model, an ongoing monthly fee that begins after the guarantee period so this stops being a one-and-done project and starts being the recurring business you already know how to build.

## Part 8: Putting the ten pieces in order

None of the sections above tell you what to build first. Here's the sequence, borrowing the "rent the hard layer, own the layer clients see" split from your real estate playbook:

1. **Foundation (week 1-2):** ICP definition and offer document, landing page and consent-capturing lead funnel, Warmly/Bullseye account setup, A2P and disclosure pages live before anything else goes out.
2. **The content engine (week 2-4):** pick one production path (A, B, C, or D) as your default, keep the others as upsells, and get to a repeatable weekly video output before promising 90 in 90.
3. **The research-to-outreach loop (week 3-5):** connect deanonymization to a scoring rule against the ICP, then to a personalized email sequence. This is your genuinely original piece; get it working before anything else scales.
4. **The booking layer (week 4-6):** chatbot on the landing page, phone receptionist for inbound and callback only (Correction 4), both wired to the same calendar as the outreach sequence.
5. **The dashboard (week 5-8):** start as a real-time view of leads, calls booked, and appointment status; only build the "instruct it in plain language" layer once the underlying data is trustworthy, an agentic layer on top of bad data is worse than no dashboard at all.
6. **First pilot client (week 6-10):** run the whole loop for one real client before selling a second, time every step, and administer the Sean Ellis question from Part 6 once their guarantee period ends.
